# Beyond Better Seeds: Why Food Systems Need Broader Change

New documents from a global summit in Bhutan explore how leadership, policies and collaboration can complement agricultural innovation. In September, Seed World Europe reported on research into deeper rooted soybeans ( https://www.seedworld.com/europe/2026/09/22/deeper-rooted-soybeans-field-trials/ ), exploring whether improved root systems could help crops withstand drought and store more carbon in agricultural soils. But the research also raised a broader question: how can promising agricultural innovations successfully reach farmers? Andrew Bovarnick of the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), quoted in that article, highlighted the importance of policies, financing, incentives and market conditions in determining whether new technologies are adopted. That broader perspective also featured at the first Global Conscious Food Systems Summit, held in Bhutan from 31 August to 4 September 2026. Bringing together nearly 400 participants from around 65 countries, the summit explored how leadership, values, trust and cooperation can complement scientific and technical solutions in transforming food systems. Its outcomes were subsequently brought into discussions at the UN Committee on World Food Security in Rome in October. The organisers have now made two documents available for those interested in exploring the outcomes: 1. [Seven Shifts to Transform Our Food Systems: A Call to Action (PDF)]( https://consciousfoodsystems.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026.11.18_CoFSA_Call-to-Action.pdf ) 2. [Global Conscious Food Systems Summit Report 2026 (PDF)]( https://consciousfoodsystems.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CoFSA-Summit-Report-2026.pdf )
