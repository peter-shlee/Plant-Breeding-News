# Breeders Trust Sues Over Undeclared Farm-Saved Seed Potatoes

Breeders Trust has sued a West Flanders potato grower, alleging the grower failed to declare between 545 and 776 hectares planted with farm-saved seed potatoes of six protected varieties. The Brussels-based organisation announced on 23 November that it had filed the case in the Commercial Court of Ghent. It alleges the grower, who has operated under several company names in recent years, infringed plant breeders’ rights for the varieties Alegria, Artemis, Challenger, Fontane, Innovator and Markies. Before filing the lawsuit, Breeders Trust obtained court permission to seize the grower’s records. The organisation said gaps in the crop records made it difficult to establish the exact area planted and that it relied on a forensic expert’s report to help estimate the area, according to a press release. Under an agreement with the Belgian agricultural sector, growers who correctly declare their use of farm-saved seed potatoes of protected varieties pay 50% of the standard licence fee, said Corné van Beers, director of Breeders Trust. He said the full fee becomes due if a grower is found to have failed to declare the acreage. Breeders Trust is seeking more than €165,000 in damages. Van Beers said more growers are reporting their use of farm-saved seed potatoes by the 1 June deadline, and that declared acreage rose 10% from the previous season. He added that some traders and processors refuse to buy potatoes grown from undeclared farm-saved seed of protected varieties. Breeders Trust plans to continue checking growers’ compliance with the reporting requirement in 2027.
