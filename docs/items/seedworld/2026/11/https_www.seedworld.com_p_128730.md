# EU-UK SPS Agreement to Ease Seed Trade and Variety Listing

The two sides aim to apply the agreement from 1 March 2028. It would reopen the EU market to seed potatoes from Great Britain and simplify seed trade and variety listing. The European Union and the United Kingdom have concluded negotiations on a plant and animal health agreement that could ease the movement of seeds and other plant products across the Channel. Leaders announced the outcome at their 20 November summit in Brussels. The sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement would establish a common area with joint health rules and certain common quality standards. Under the deal, the “vast majority” of movements of plants, plant products, animals and animal products between the EU and Great Britain and the Crown Dependencies could take place “without the certificates or controls that are currently required,” according to the UK–EU summit joint statement . The same benefits would extend to shipments between Great Britain and Northern Ireland through the Windsor Framework. Northern Ireland would retain access to both the UK internal market and the EU single market. What Would Change for Seed Trade and Breeding? The EU market would reopen to seed potatoes from Great Britain, and companies would no longer need OECD certification to market seed between Great Britain and the EU. Phytosanitary certificates would be dropped for trade between the two markets, and routine border checks would end for eligible shipments. Customs controls would continue. Seed potatoes entering Great Britain from the EU would require advance notification and could be checked at their destination as per the UK government horticulture guidance . Breeders could apply to list a variety in an EU member state or in Great Britain. A variety accepted onto the Great Britain Variety List would also be added to the EU’s Common Catalogue. For new applications filed after the agreement takes effect, a single Community Plant Variety Right could protect a variety across the UK and the EU, according to the UK guidance on breeding plants, seeds and crops . Businesses in Great Britain would also switch to EU plant passports, with the requirement extending to additional seed types, including rice and Allium species such as onions. The UK government plans to publish more detailed guidance for edible and ornamental horticulture businesses in spring 2027. See the UK guidance on plant passports and implementation for more information.
