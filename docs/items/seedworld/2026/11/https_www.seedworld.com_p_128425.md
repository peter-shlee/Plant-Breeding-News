# We Bought a Seed Plant. The Support Came With It.

When we bought a neighbouring farm a few years ago in Lajord, Sask., the seed cleaning plant came with it. I knew we could use it, but I didn’t really know what I was getting myself into. With the addition of that land, our operation grew to around 35,000 acres. We clean roughly 60,000 to 70,000 bushels of seed annually just for ourselves, so having our own facility made sense. The challenge was figuring out how to run it . I remember walking into that plant and looking at all the equipment. My first thought was, how am I supposed to set all these machines properly? Once the grain starts flowing, it goes through the entire plant. You’re adjusting one machine while three others are running, and you don’t necessarily know whether they’re set correctly. Fortunately, the equipment was Cimbria machinery supplied through Nexeed. We hadn’t purchased it from Nexeed ourselves, but that didn’t seem to matter. They were there to help us figure it out. The gravity table was probably the biggest learning curve. You’ve got vertical tilt, lateral tilt, air speed and vibratory speed. Four different things you’re trying to get right. When I connected with Rod Cockerline and Greg Coulter from Nexeed, they gave me some basic starting settings that made a big difference. But the support didn’t stop there. As I became more comfortable operating the plant, I started wanting to do more myself. The colour sorter was a good example. I didn’t want to call someone every time I needed to make a simple adjustment. I wanted to understand how to build my own recipes. Bob Salnykov, Nexeed’s colour sorter specialist, helped me with that process. And when I needed help, Nexeed was available. Their technicians can connect remotely to help troubleshoot and fine-tune the equipment. Having that kind of backup gives you confidence to learn what the machinery can do. That’s been the biggest benefit for me. I like figuring things out myself, but knowing there’s someone who understands the equipment and can help when I need it makes a difference. We inherited this plant when we bought the farm. What I didn’t necessarily expect was that we’d inherit a relationship with the people who supplied it, too. And that relationship has been every bit as valuable as the equipment itself.
