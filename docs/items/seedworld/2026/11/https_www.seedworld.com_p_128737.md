# Do We Want a Say in What Different Looks Like? | On The Brink: Season 3, Episode 12

CropLife Canada’s Ian Affleck says the plant breeding sector needs a shared plan for research funding and regulation if it wants to help shape what comes next. Canada’s plant breeding sector needs to agree on what it wants from government and offer a practical way to get there, says Ian Affleck of CropLife Canada. In Season 3, Episode 12 of On The Brink, Affleck, vice president of plant biotechnology and sustainability, argues that the sector is already experiencing change. The task now is deciding how to manage it. “Tomorrow will be different,” he says. “The question is, do we wanna have a say in what different looks like?” An opportunity to shape plant breeding regulation Affleck points to the federal government’s focus on competitiveness and productivity, along with regulatory directives and red tape reduction initiatives, as reasons for the sector to act. He sees what he describes as a generational opportunity to ask for a regulatory system that delivers quality varieties without inhibiting innovation or leaving Canada behind international competitors. That discussion includes the regulation of biotechnology traits, such as GMOs and gene editing, as well as the variety registration system. Affleck wants the sector to consider how those processes can maintain quality while allowing new varieties to reach the market. Connecting research funding and investment Regulation is one part of the opportunity Affleck describes. The other is how government funding moves through the plant breeding system and supports public and private research. He argues that a modern regulatory system could help attract foreign investment into seed companies conducting research in Canada. He also sees potential for partnerships with universities that build on work already completed in public research programs. A shared plan needs growers at the table Affleck says the discussion needs to include grower groups, whose members use the varieties and whose checkoff dollars often support research programs. Public researchers, seed growers, end users and private sector breeders also need to help develop the vision. “If government’s going to pick up on a new model, it’s gotta be one that’s supported across the board.” Turning a request into a path forward Affleck cautions that the opening he sees may not last. It could extend for one government cycle or two, making it important for the sector to organize its priorities while the opportunity exists. “If the industry doesn’t come up with clear asks of the government and put them on their table with a way to achieve them, they won’t happen,” he says.
