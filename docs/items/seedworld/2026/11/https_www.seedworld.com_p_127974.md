# Preparing European Agriculture For a Climate it Can’t Predict

The summer of 2026 brought record-breaking heat waves, historic droughts and unprecedented wildfires to many European countries, resulting in significant crop reductions and economic strife across the continent. It also highlighted something we’ve known for years — climate change is going to have wide-ranging consequences for agriculture that the seed industry needs to help address. Greater volatility and weather extremes will continue to impact everything from the timing when crops are planted and harvested to disease pressure, seed quality and yields. Some crops or varieties that are widely grown throughout Europe will likely need to shift northwards. Meanwhile, growers in southern regions may find themselves abandoning traditional crops for other ones that are better adapted to our new climate reality. Staphyt is a contract research organization (CRO) with 25 self-sufficient experimental seed stations located in 12 European countries that together provide access to an extensive network of field sites comprising over 300,000 microplots. We are capable of managing trials that conform to strict standards. And each of our stations is equipped with high-quality, innovative equipment that allows us to manage everything from small local seed trials to large projects for international breeding companies and seed distributors. Our services are diverse. While pre-registration and post-registration variety trials make up the bulk of our business, we also have the capacity to manage seed multiplication and production, line screenings, disease, stress, density and seed treatment trials, and more. Our field teams are experts in what they do and are always ready to adapt to the situation on a season by season basis and to change course, as necessary, to aid our clients in finding the best way forward. Twenty years ago, those clients were focused primarily on crop yields. More recently, their needs have shifted towards agronomy, and we have shifted with them. While the bulk of our work continues to involve variety trials, we are seeing growing interest in abiotic stress tests, new crop types and new techniques. The last decade has also seen us adopt new technologies, allowing us to assess crops using drones, satellite imagery and other cutting-edge tools. Last year we had the opportunity to take another step into the future by acquiring France-based Héliantis Group Subsidiaries, making us the top CRO in Europe. The industry-leading expertise at Héliantis has opened a whole new world of possibilities for our local and global clients, expanding our plant breed, stress testing, data collection and, most importantly, our seed production services. We measure our success by the faith our clients place in the quality of our services. When I see the same organizations coming back to the same location with the same program year after year, that gives me confidence that we are offering quality services that are helping our clients succeed now and will continue to do so well into the future.
