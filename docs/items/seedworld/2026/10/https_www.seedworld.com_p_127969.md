# More Genetics, More Markets: Agri Obtentions Looks Beyond France

New opportunities are opening for European seed companies to access Agri Obtentions’ genetics as the French breeder looks beyond France for its next stage of growth. The INRAE subsidiary is expanding into additional European markets through local partnerships, bringing a portfolio spanning 15 field-crop species to seed businesses that already know their customers and production systems. By combining that local market knowledge with Agri Obtentions’ genetics, research and breeding capacity, the partnerships can open new possibilities for varieties and crops that fit emerging needs in individual markets. “The opportunity is different in every market,” says François Cuvelier, sales director for France and export. “The question is where our genetics can strengthen what a seed company is already offering and help it respond to where demand is moving.” Agri Obtentions has strengthened its export team, with Paul Debaut now covering half of Southern Europe alongside François. Emerging Opportunities As European rotations diversify, seed companies may need a broader range of crop and varietal solutions. Triticale is a good example. In France, triticale is the third-largest straw cereal, with acreage growing from approximately 255,000 hectares in 2024 to a projected 312,000 hectares in 2026, according to Agreste. Its relatively low nitrogen requirement may add to that appeal. According to Arvalis, triticale requires approximately 2.6 kg of nitrogen per quintal, compared with about 3 kg for bread wheat. Agri Obtentions brings more than 40 years of triticale breeding experience to that opportunity, with significant varietal renewal in recent years. The company also points to grain yield, straw production, performance on difficult soils and suitability for low-input and organic systems as reasons for partners to consider where the crop could fit in their markets. Paul Debaut and François Cuvelier, Export Sales Department Partners looking for new ways to respond to Europe’s push for greater protein production may find another opportunity in winter pea and winter faba bean. Even where protein-crop (pea, faba beans and lentils) acre­age remains under pressure, Agri Obtentions continues to invest in genetics that could give partners more options as growing conditions shift. Climate change may strengthen the case for winter types: autumn sowing can help crops make better use of available water, spread production risk and avoid some late-cy­cle stresses. “We’re looking for places where our genetics can answer a real market need,” François says. “That starts with understanding what growers are facing locally and where our breeding programs can bring something useful to the portfolio.” A Long View on Innovation Behind those partnerships is more than four decades of varietal development. Founded in 1983, Agri Obtentions has approximately 60 employees and €15 million in turnover across France and Europe. It has released more than 450 varieties, works with more than 400 licensees and reinvests approximately 30% of revenue in R&D. The company believes seed is the first vector of each country’s sovereignty, and that varietal innovation, kept accessible to all through Plant Breeders’ Rights (the French « certification d’obtention variétale » COV), is what gives the seed business a better future. “Seed is the starting point for a country’s agricultural sovereignty,” François says. “Our role as breeders is to keep creating varietal innovation that gives farmers and seed companies more options for the challenges ahead.”
