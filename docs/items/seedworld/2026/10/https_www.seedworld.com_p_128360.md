# Could Better Market Intelligence Change How Seed Companies Choose Genetics?

Seed companies have become highly sophisticated at understanding where and under what conditions genetics will perform well. At Ahern, we conduct trials across environments, evaluate traits under different production conditions, and develop increasingly precise recommendations on where varieties fit best. That expertise is the foundation of our work, but it’s just the first step in a vision that extends beyond field performance. In vegetables, a variety’s performance is only one part of the equation. The crop still needs to be harvested, packed, transported, and sold in a market where demand, prices, and competitive conditions are constantly changing. Ultimately, growers need that entire equation to work. That is why our role as a strategic partner requires us to understand both where to position a variety and the conditions that make the business around it viable and profitable. We conduct approximately 2,500 trials each year. Our experimental station in Culiacán allows us to monitor varieties throughout the production cycle while evaluating different technologies and production practices. At the same time, our close relationships with growers help us understand how water availability, labor, and financial pressures influence their decisions. We also see how logistics affect the costs and profitability of moving perishable products between regions, and we hear when growers begin considering a different crop or segment because their business conditions have changed. By working with multiple seed companies, growers, and regions, we can put those signals into context, distinguish isolated situations from broader trends, and identify implications for the commercial development of varieties. The opportunity lies in connecting what happens in marketing, distribution, and consumption more directly with decisions about which genetics to bring to market, where to position them, and how portfolios should evolve. As a link between seed companies and growers, Ahern plays a key role in making that connection. A variety may continue to perform exactly as expected while the commercial opportunity around it changes. A shift in trade conditions can alter the attractiveness of one crop relative to another. Changes in consumer demand can create greater value in a particular segment. Rising production or logistics costs can reshape growers’ investment priorities. New disease pressures can suddenly make a trait critically important. Genetic development operates on much longer timelines, and that discipline is essential. This makes timely market insight especially valuable in distinguishing temporary fluctuations from changes that should influence product positioning, investment in market development, and, ultimately, portfolio decisions. Our contribution is to gather evidence, identify patterns, and share useful insights with our partners so they can make better-informed decisions earlier.This ability to bring knowledge together, interpret change, and guide action strengthens our role as a strategic partner across the value chain. We are becoming increasingly precise in anticipating how a variety will perform in a given environment. Our competitive advantage lies in understanding how the business surrounding that variety is evolving and bringing that knowledge to decision makers in time to make a difference. This is the vision driving Ahern’s leadership in North America: connecting genetics, production, and markets to help build a more competitive value chain and support the growth of our growers and business partners.
